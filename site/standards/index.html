What AS 1288 means
for your glazing.

Glass is selected against the opening it goes into — its size, its height above the floor, what is above it and what it has to resist. Not against a product brochure.

Selection, not preference.

AS 1288 is the standard that decides what glass is permitted in a given location in a building. It works from measurable things: the area of the pane, the height of the bottom edge above the floor, whether it is in a door or beside one, whether people are likely to walk into it, and the loads — wind, human impact, or what sits above.

Two consequences follow, and both matter commercially. First, a quote that names a glass "type" without a thickness is not a quote for a compliant installation. Second, replacing broken glass like-for-like is not automatically correct — the requirement belongs to the location, not the old pane.

What we record on every job

  • The application and the measured panel size
  • The make-up and nominal thickness supplied
  • The standard the selection was made under
  • Who measured, supplied and installed

NotesStandards explained
What is AS 1288?
AS 1288 is the Australian Standard for glass in buildings — selection and installation. It sets out how thick glass must be, and whether it must be safety glass, based on the panel size, the height above the floor, the location in the building and the loads it has to resist.
What is Grade A safety glass?
Grade A safety glass is toughened or laminated glass that meets AS 2208. If it breaks, toughened glass breaks into small blunt fragments and laminated glass stays in its frame rather than falling as large shards.
Why can't one thickness suit every job?
Because the requirement scales with area and height. A 600mm panel low in a wall and a 2.4m panel in the same wall can need different make-ups, and both are legal. The standard is applied to the actual opening, not to the product name.
What does a glazing certificate certify?
It identifies the job, the glass supplied, its make-up and thickness, the standard it was selected under, and who installed it. It is a record of what was installed — not a warranty of the building.
Does this page specify the glass for my project?
No. This is an explanation of how selection works. The specification for your job comes from the measured panel, its location and the applicable loads — which is what the quote and certificate record.
