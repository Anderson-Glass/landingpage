Toughened or laminated?

Two safety glasses that behave completely differently when they break.

Guide520 words

What each one does when it fails

Toughened glass is heated and rapidly cooled so the outer surfaces are in compression. When it breaks it releases into thousands of small blunt pieces — which is why it is used where somebody might walk into it. It is strong against impact but it does not stay in the opening once broken: the whole panel goes.

Laminated glass is two or more sheets bonded to a plastic interlayer. When it breaks the crack runs but the pieces stay attached to the interlayer. It stays in the frame, keeps the weather out and keeps the opening closed — which is why it is used where falling glass or staying secure matters.

Where each one belongs

Doors, door side panels and low glass in residential buildings are the classic toughened locations: the concern is a person going through it. Overhead glazing, balustrades, shopfronts in some situations and anywhere the glass is above people or needs to stay in place fall on the laminated side, because the concern is what comes down.

There are also locations where the requirement is both — and in those cases the make-up is laminated glass made from toughened sheets. That is not a premium upsell; it is the only way to satisfy two different requirements at once.

Why "safety glass" is not a product

Safety glass is a category, not a thing you can order. A supplier who says "it's safety glass" has told you nothing you can check. The useful sentence names the make-up and the thickness and the standard it was selected under.

This is the reason we put those three things in writing on the quote. If a job is ever inspected, or a certificate is needed, the paperwork has to match what is actually in the opening.

What this means when something breaks

A broken toughened panel usually means the entire panel is gone — there is nothing to patch. A broken laminated panel may still be holding the weather out while you wait for the replacement. Knowing which one you have changes what you do in the first hour.

If the broken glass is in a door, a low window, or anywhere a child could reach, treat the opening as unsafe until it is made safe, even if the remaining glass looks intact.

In short
  • Toughened shatters into blunt pieces; laminated stays in the frame
  • Location decides which one — not budget, and not preference
  • "Safety glass" is a category; the make-up and standard are what count
  • Laminated-from-toughened exists where both behaviours are required

QuestionsToughened or laminated?
Which is stronger?
They resist different things. Toughened takes a harder direct impact before it fails; laminated stays in the opening after it fails. Stronger is the wrong question — the right one is what the glass has to do if it breaks.
Can I have normal glass if it is high up?
Height is only one of the factors. Being above head height may remove the human-impact concern but add a falling-glass one, which points at laminated rather than ordinary glass.
Do I need a certificate?
Some jobs require glazing certification naming what was supplied. We provide certificates where a job requires one, and the quote says so up front rather than after the invoice.
